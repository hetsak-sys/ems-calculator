# Hetsa PowerSuite — Marketing & Sales Strategy
## Written for whoever sells this, not just for Hertz

**How to use this document:** if you're briefing a salesperson who isn't an electrical engineer,
this doc is the thing to hand them. It explains what the product is, who buys it, why they buy it,
what to say, and what NOT to promise. Anywhere a number is missing (price, commission %), that's
deliberate — those are Hertz's calls, flagged clearly below rather than guessed.

---

## 1. What PowerSuite actually is (the 30-second version)

PowerSuite is an Android app that replaces engineering reference books, manual calculations, and
error-prone spreadsheets for electrical technicians, engineers, and mine electrical staff working in
South Africa and Lesotho. It runs entirely offline — no signal needed on site — and every
calculation is grounded in a named standard (IEC, SANS, IEEE, MHSA), not a generic formula.

**One sentence for a cold pitch:** *"It's the reference book and the calculator, combined, that
actually knows the standards you work to — and it works with zero signal, on a once-off payment,
no subscription."*

## 2. Who buys this, and why (segment by segment)

| Segment | Why they buy | What matters most to them |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual field technicians/engineers** | Replaces a stack of reference books and error-prone manual math; saves time on-site | Offline reliability, once-off price (not a subscription), covers the standards they actually work to |
| **Contractors (small-medium electrical firms)** | Standardizes calculations across a small team; reduces rework from sizing errors | Consistency across staff, PDF export for client hand-over/compliance records, cost predictability |
| **Mines** | Field conditions are extreme (dust, gloves, no signal, high-risk work); MHSA compliance matters directly | MHSA-referenced content, ruggedness of the offline model, protection/earthing/arc-flash modules specifically |
| **Training colleges** | Wants students trained on a real tool they'll actually use on the job, not a toy | Institutional bulk licensing, seat management as students graduate, credibility of the standards base |
| **Consulting engineers** | Needs traceable, standards-cited working for sign-off | Every result names its clause/standard; PDF export as a working record |

**The unifying thread across every segment:** this market actively resists subscription software.
A once-off purchase is not just a pricing choice — it's the single biggest reason someone in this
market will trust a new tool at all. Lead with "no subscription, ever" every time.

## 3. What actually makes this different (the real differentiators — use these, they're true)

Don't reach for generic SaaS marketing language ("game-changing," "revolutionary"). Everything below
is a specific, checkable fact about the product — that's the actual sales strength here.

1. **Offline-first, not "offline-capable."** Every calculation works with zero connectivity — not a
   degraded mode, not a cache. The only thing that ever needs internet is the license check, and only
   briefly. On a mine or a rural site with no signal, this isn't a nice-to-have.
2. **Once-off pricing.** No subscription, ever. In a market that has burned out on subscription
   fatigue, this alone is a door-opener.
3. **Named-standard grounding, not generic formulas.** Every module cites the actual clause it's
   built on (SANS 10142, IEC 60909, IEEE 80, MHSA regs, etc.) — and where a figure genuinely isn't
   verifiable against a real standard, the app says so honestly rather than guessing. That honesty is
   itself a trust signal worth stating out loud to a skeptical buyer, especially an engineer who'll
   actually check.
4. **Built by a working electrical technician, for this specific market.** Not a generic global
   engineering app repackaged for the region — genuinely built around South African/Lesotho standards,
   altitude conditions, and field realities from day one.
5. **Broad, real coverage.** Motors, cable sizing, earthing, protection coordination (including a
   full time-current coordination study builder), power systems, power quality, renewable energy
   (PV/battery/grid-tie), installation design, and overhead reticulation — genuinely deep modules, not
   a shallow calculator wrapper.
6. **Institutional licensing that respects how institutions actually work.** Bulk seats, reassignable
   as staff/students turn over — not "one license, one person, forever."

## 4. Objection handling — what a buyer will actually push back with

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "I can just use Excel / my own spreadsheets." | Fair — but every formula is manually re-entered and re-trusted every time, with no built-in check against the actual standard. One transposed cell reference in a fault-level calc is a real risk. PowerSuite is the same math, already verified against the standard, with derating/checks built in automatically. |
| "There are free calculator apps already." | Generic apps aren't grounded in the standards this market actually works to (SANS/IEC/MHSA), and most assume signal is available. Ask what standard their current app cites — usually the honest answer is "none in particular." |
| "Why pay once-off instead of a cheap monthly fee?" | Once-off means it's yours — no risk of losing access if a subscription lapses on a remote site with no signal to renew it anyway. |
| "How do I know the license won't just vanish if I switch phones?" | The self-service reactivation flow handles a device change directly in the app — no waiting on support for the common case. |
| "What if I lend my phone / someone leaves the company?" | This is exactly why institutional licensing exists — it's a seat that belongs to the organization, reassignable as people come and go, not tied to one person's device forever. |

## 5. Pricing structure (framework — actual numbers are Hertz's decision)

This document deliberately does **not** invent price points — that's a real business decision
outside marketing strategy. What follows is the *shape* of the pricing model already established,
which a salesperson needs to understand and explain correctly:

- **Trial:** 90 days, free, tracked server-side (so it can't be reset by reinstalling).
- **Standard license:** once-off purchase, one key per person, bound to one device at a time, with
  self-service device-swap (2 per rolling 30 days) if they change phones.
- **Institutional license:** bulk purchase of N seats by an organization (mine, contractor, college).
  Seats belong to the institution, not the individual — reassignable when someone leaves, via a
  manual request to Hertz directly (not self-service — this is deliberate, see the developer
  quickref for why).

**What a salesperson needs from Hertz before quoting anyone:** the actual once-off price for a
standard key, and the institutional bulk-rate structure (flat per-seat, volume discount tiers, or a
minimum seat count). Don't estimate these — get them from Hertz directly and keep this doc updated
once they're set.

## 6. Sales channels, ranked by expected effort-to-return for this market

1. **Direct relationships in existing networks** — mining/electrical contractor WhatsApp groups,
   trade associations, local electrical supply stores that would let you leave a flyer/demo QR code.
   Lowest cost, likely highest trust, since this is a relationship-driven market.
2. **College/training institution partnerships** — approach electrical engineering departments at
   technical colleges directly with an institutional trial offer. This is a slower sales cycle but a
   high-value, high-retention segment (an entire cohort learning on the tool they'll later buy
   individually or bring into their first employer).
3. **WhatsApp Status / social presence** — see the companion flyer scripts. Low cost, good fit for
   how this market actually communicates day-to-day.
4. **Mining-sector trade shows / MHSA-related events** — higher cost, but direct access to the buyers
   who care most about compliance-referenced tooling.
5. **Referral incentive** — existing satisfied users are a strong channel in a trust-driven market;
   a small referral credit/discount is worth considering once pricing is set (Hertz's call).

## 7. The demo script (what to actually show a prospect)

Don't try to show every module — pick ONE that matches the prospect's actual work and let them see
themselves in it:

- **Field technician/contractor:** show Motor FLA → Cable Sizing hand-off (the FLA auto-carries into
  cable sizing — a genuinely satisfying "it just works" moment) and then Export PDF, so they see a
  real hand-over document come out the other end.
- **Mine electrical staff:** show Protection's Coord. Study (the full time-current coordination
  builder with the log-log plot) or the Arc Flash estimate — both speak directly to safety-critical
  daily work.
- **Solar/renewable installer:** show Renewable Energy's Array sizing (the cold/hot Voc/Vmp
  compatibility check is a genuinely useful "catches a real mistake before it happens" moment) or
  Grid-Tie's NRS 097-2 compliance checklist.
- **College:** show the breadth — flip through several modules quickly to convey "this is the whole
  curriculum's worth of calculation, in one tool," then talk institutional licensing.

Always close the demo by starting the 90-day trial live, on their own phone, before you leave the
conversation. A started trial converts far better than a promise to "try it later."

## 8. What NOT to promise (guardrails for a non-technical salesperson)

- Never claim a figure is verified against a standard if you're not sure — several modules are
  explicit about being an estimate or a general reference rather than a full engineering study (Arc
  Flash, Grading table, some Area Lighting categories). If a prospect asks a technical detail you're
  not sure of, say "let me confirm that" rather than guessing — this market will check.
- Don't promise features that don't exist yet or are explicitly out of scope (e.g. Overhead
  Reticulation deliberately does not do sag/tension mechanical calculations — that's flagged in the
  app itself, don't oversell past it).
- Don't quote institutional pricing or seat-reassignment turnaround times without confirming current
  policy with Hertz first — the reassignment process is currently a manual, email-based request, not
  instant self-service.
